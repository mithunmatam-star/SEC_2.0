How it works

Three steps to a sharper explanation

No flashcards, no passive videos. You build understanding by producing it out loud — in text.

01

Pick a topic and a persona

Choose your subject and topic, then decide who you're teaching: a Beginner, a Curious Kid, a Skeptic, or a Professor.

02

Teach the AI student

The AI asks one short follow-up question at a time. It never lectures you back, so every gap has to be filled by your own explanation.

03

Get your evaluation

End the session to receive scores for accuracy, clarity, completeness, and examples — plus misconceptions, missing concepts, and next steps.

The Feynman technique

If you can't explain it simply, you don't know it yet.

Richard Feynman's learning method is deceptively simple: explain the idea in plain language, notice where you get stuck, then go back and study that part.

1

Choose a concept

Start with one specific topic instead of a whole chapter.

2

Explain it in simple language

Write as if your listener knows nothing. No jargon shortcuts.

3

Find the gaps

Confused questions are signals. Every one points to a weak spot.

4

Refine and re-teach

Study the gap, then teach it again and compare your scores.
